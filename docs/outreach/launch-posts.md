## Longer English post

AI is developing faster than one person can understand alone, and I keep coming back to a question that is easy to ask badly:

If an artificial agent lives through a sequence of choices and consequences, remembers what happened, and cannot simply replace its past with a fresh prompt, can a durable identity-like pattern emerge?

Emergent Self Lab is my attempt to study that question in public.

The project will begin with identical agents placed on controlled, different experience paths. Later, they will face blinded tasks designed to test preference stability, history dependence, consequence sensitivity, and whether their behavior survives harmless changes in wording.

There are important limits. A system saying “I am conscious” is not proof of consciousness. A persistent behavioral pattern is not automatically a mind. The project will also keep some core implementation details private because the open research layer is not the same thing as publishing every piece of intellectual property.

What I can promise is narrower: careful experiments, honest uncertainty, and a record that includes failed results as well as exciting ones.

If you know a better control, a hidden confound, or a simpler explanation, that is exactly the kind of contribution I want.

Emergent Self Lab 是我想公開做這件事的起點。

第一步會從幾個起點完全相同的人工代理開始，讓它們走過不同但受控制的經驗路徑。之後再用盲測方式觀察：偏好是否穩定、後續行為是否依賴歷史、遇到不同後果時是否真的改變，以及換個說法後這些模式是否仍然存在。

這裡有幾條界線必須先講清楚：系統說「我有意識」不是有意識的證明；穩定的行為模式也不等於心智。我也不會把核心身份引擎、私有方法、憑證或敏感資料全部公開，因為公開研究層不等於放棄所有核心智慧財產。
