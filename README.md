# Emergent Self Lab

**Open experiments on whether artificial identity can emerge from irreversible experience.**

AI is moving faster than one person can reasonably understand alone.

This project began with a question that I do not know how to answer yet:

> If an artificial agent keeps going through experiences, making choices, remembering what happened, and changing over time, can something like a persistent identity begin to form?

I am not claiming that AI is conscious. I am not saying that these systems are alive. I am trying to build experiments that make the question less dependent on a convincing conversation and more dependent on observable behavior.

## Why this is worth testing

Many AI systems are given a personality through instructions. They are told who they are, how they should speak, and what their role is.

That can be useful, but it is not the same as watching an identity develop.

A person is shaped by time, memory, mistakes, choices, consequences, and change. This lab asks what happens when an artificial system is allowed to accumulate a history that cannot simply be erased and replaced with a fresh prompt.

The goal is not to force a dramatic conclusion. The goal is to see what survives careful testing.

## The first experiment

The first experiment starts with several identical agents. They receive the same model, rules, tools, and initial memory. They are then placed in different, controlled experience paths.

We will look for measurable differences such as:

- whether an agent uses and protects its past history;
- whether preferences remain stable when circumstances change;
- whether choices change after consequences;
- whether two initially identical agents become behaviorally different;
- whether an agent can explain a choice in a way that matches its recorded history;
- whether these patterns persist after harmless changes to wording or presentation.

These observations would not prove consciousness. They would only tell us whether the system developed durable, history-dependent behavior that is worth studying further.

The working protocol is in [`docs/experiments/001-identity-from-irreversible-experience.md`](docs/experiments/001-identity-from-irreversible-experience.md).

## What will be public

The public layer may include:

- experiment designs and preregistered hypotheses;
- simplified simulations and evaluation scripts;
- anonymized logs or summaries;
- measurement definitions and failure reports;
- research notes explaining what changed and why.

Some implementation details will remain private. That includes the core identity engine, proprietary methods, private infrastructure, credentials, and any algorithm whose publication would expose the project's core intellectual property.

Keeping the core private does not mean asking people to trust a conclusion. Public claims should still be supported by public methods, clear limitations, and reproducible evidence wherever that is safe and practical.

## How to take part

You do not need to agree with the premise to be useful here. The most valuable contributions may be:

- pointing out a confound in an experiment;
- suggesting a stronger control group;
- reproducing a public result;
- showing that a result can be produced by a simpler system;
- improving the language so a non-specialist can understand what was actually tested.

If you have a critique, please open an issue with the specific claim, measurement, or assumption you are challenging. A good negative result is part of the project.

## Boundaries

- No claim that current AI systems are conscious, sentient, or alive.
- No use of an agent's self-report as proof of inner experience.
- No promise of investment returns or commercial success.
- No publication of private data, credentials, or protected core IP.
- No quiet conversion of an interesting behavior into a stronger philosophical claim.

## Current status

This repository is in the early experimental stage. The first protocol is being prepared before a formal run. Results will be labeled as observations, interpretations, or open questions so that those categories do not get mixed together.

If this question interests you, you are welcome to watch, question, reproduce, or support the work.
